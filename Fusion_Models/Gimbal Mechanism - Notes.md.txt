**Software:** Autodesk Fusion 360
  
**Date Completed:** 11/20/25 

**Objective:** To model a gimballed engine mount designed for model rocketry for the pedagogical purposes
of applying functional joints and growing in my understanding of assemblies.

**Results:** Working joints were not effectively implemented, due primarily to messy sketching, poor labelling,
and general disorganization resulting in downstream difficulties when particular entites had to be selected for 
joint application. Many sketches were simply sketched over instead of edited, unlabelled, and not clearly dimensioned,
which slowed productivity. Many bodies listed under parent components should have been their own componenets, which
would have allowed joints to be applied with little trouble. However, by failing to be attentive to which entities
were activated when creating different pieces of the model, many parent child relationships were created when proper
design intent would have created clearly labelled and separated components for easy access. This is why there are many 
copies of bodies changed into components in the design tree: components, not bodies, were needed for the joints. From 
this I learned that it is indispensible to label every relavent entity carefully, and to take the time to fully think 
through the design beforehand. This sort of prototyping seems to bring with it a much better understanding of how 
components and bodies should be organized as they are created, which systematically avoids the scattered labelling and 
nesting of bodies and components that caused most of the issues in this project. The goal was not to create a mechanism
that would function properly in the real world, but rather one that would increase my understanding of joints and
assemblies. However, I tried to be cognizent of as many realistic limitations as possible without losing sight of the 
stated objective, and a bit of useful experience was gained 
simply from the process of modelling the mechanism, a summary of which is listed below.

**Design Summary:**
SOFTWARE: Autodesk Fusion 360
DATE COMPLETED: 11/20/25

OBJECTIVE

Model a gimballed engine mount for model rocketry in order to
practice assembly organization and the implementation of
functional joints within a CAD environment.

RESULTS

Functional joints were not successfully implemented due mainly
to poor sketch organization, inconsistent labeling, and an
improper component hierarchy in the design tree.

Many sketches were recreated instead of edited, and several
entities were left unlabeled or poorly dimensioned. Because of
this, locating specific geometry during joint creation became
difficult and slowed productivity.

Additionally, many bodies that should have been created as
separate components were instead placed under parent components.
Since joints operate between components rather than bodies,
several bodies later had to be converted into components. This
led to unnecessary duplication and a disorganized assembly
structure.

These issues were largely caused by failing to consistently
verify which component was active during modeling operations,
resulting in unintended parent-child relationships.

Although the mechanism was not intended to function in 
a physically realistic way, the modeling process 
still provided some useful experience in working
with assemblies, and is detailed below. 


DESIGN SUMMARY

MOTOR MOUNT

The motor mount houses and retains the rocket engine.

a. Body Tube

A cylindrical tube that serves as the primary structure
for the motor mount.

b. Centering Rings

Two rings positioned inside the tube to hold the engine
securely in place.

c. Thrust Ring

Located at the top of the mount. It is wide enough to absorb
engine thrust while maintaining a central opening to allow
airflow.

d. Retaining Plug

Located at the base of the tube to prevent the engine from
slipping out prior to launch.

The plug threads into the tube for easy installation and
removal. Two small flanges allow it to be tightened or
loosened manually.

INNER PINS AND PIN SLEEVES

The motor mount connects to the inner gimbal ring through
a pair of pivot pins.

a. Pin Sleeves

Rigidly fixed to the motor mount.

b. Pins

The pins slide into the sleeves and are secured with three
bolts. This allows disassembly for lubrication or replacement.

c. Bearing Interface

The pins pass through bearings seated in the inner ring.
A small clearance allows lubrication and smooth rotation.

d. Flange Constraint

A flange mounted on the outside of the ring prevents the
pins from translating axially.

INNER RING

The inner ring allows the motor mount to rotate about one
axis.

The inner ring itself rotates relative to the outer ring
through a pivot arrangement similar to the one connecting
the motor mount and inner ring. This allows rotation about
a second orthogonal axis.

OUTER RING AND SERVO ASSEMBLIES

The outer ring remains fixed relative to the rocket
structure.

Each ring is actuated by a servo mechanism.

The servo rotates an arm, which is connected to a flange
attached to the pivot pin through a connecting rod. The
connecting rod is pinned at both ends, allowing rotational
freedom.

When the servo arm moves, the connecting rod pushes or
pulls the flange. This rotates the corresponding pin and
adjusts the orientation of either the inner ring or the
motor mount.

CONCLUSIONS

This project was intended to build familiarity with joint
implementation and assembly organization in Fusion 360.

Although the joints did not function correctly within the
final assembly, the exercise highlighted several critical
workflow practices:

1.) Careful sketch organization

2.) Consistent labeling of features and components

3.) Planning component hierarchy before modeling

4.) Planning ahead to create components rather than bodies when joints are
required

These lessons significantly improved my understanding of
assembly structure and design intent in parametric CAD
environments.

I have since transitioned to SOLIDWORKS to better align
with my industry goals. However, the importance of careful
component organization and deliberate design structure
will certainly remain valuable to me across many different softwares.
